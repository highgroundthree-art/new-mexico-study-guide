# New Mexico Esthetician Exam Study Guide

This guide follows the exams a New Mexico esthetics student has to pass. It is an unofficial study aid. Confirm exam rules and dates with Professional Credential Services (PCS) and the New Mexico Board of Barbers and Cosmetologists before test day.

## 1. The three exams

| Exam | Format | To pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NIC Esthetics Theory (written) | 110 multiple-choice questions, 90 minutes. 100 are scored. | Scaled score of 70 |
| NIC Esthetics Practical (hands-on) | 6 core sections plus 2 New Mexico sections, on a mannequin | Scaled score of 70 |
| New Mexico state law | Multiple choice on the Barbers and Cosmetologists Act and Board rules (16.34 NMAC) | Scaled score of 70 |

To be eligible: age 17 or older and 600 hours at a New Mexico licensed school, finished within the past 24 months.

## 2. Where the written questions come from

| Domain | Share of exam | About how many questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I. Scientific Concepts | 55% | 55 |
| II. Skin Care and Services | 45% | 45 |

Spend a little more than half of your study time on science. NIC does not publish question counts for smaller topics.

## 3. How to use this kit

1. Read one section of this guide.
2. Go through the flashcards for that topic at Level 1. Move up a level when the cards feel easy.
3. Take the Level 1 quiz. At 18 or more correct out of 25 (70%), move to the next level.
4. Missed a question? Find that term in the flashcards and read all four levels.
5. Level 4 is the closest to the real exam. Aim for 80% or better there before booking the test.

## 4. Domain I: Scientific Concepts (55%)

### Microbiology

- **Bacteria:** one-celled organisms. Nonpathogenic bacteria are harmless. Pathogenic bacteria cause disease.
- **Viruses:** multiply only inside a host's cells. Antibiotics don't work on them. Examples: herpes simplex, hepatitis, HIV.
- **Fungi:** molds and yeasts. Tinea (ringworm) is a contagious fungal infection.
- **Parasites:** live on or in a host. Examples: head lice, scabies mites.

### Infection control

| Level | What it does | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sanitation (cleaning) | Removes dirt and lowers the number of germs | Always the first step |
| Disinfection | Kills most germs, but not bacterial spores | Hard, nonporous tools and surfaces |
| Sterilization | Kills all microbes, including spores | Anything that breaks the skin |

- Clean first, then disinfect. Disinfectant can't work through dirt and oil.
- Use an EPA-registered disinfectant and keep the item wet for the full **contact time** on the label.
- Disinfectants are for tools and surfaces. Antiseptics are for skin.
- Porous or single-use items (cotton, wooden sticks, sponges) are thrown away after one client.
- Replace disinfectant when it is cloudy or dirty.

### Safety

- **Standard Precautions:** treat all blood and body fluids as infectious, for every client.
- **Blood exposure procedure:** stop the service, put on gloves, clean the wound with antiseptic, bandage it, double-bag blood-soiled items, disinfect tools and the station, remove gloves and wash hands.
- **Safety Data Sheet (SDS):** the manufacturer's document for each chemical product. It lists hazards, storage, protective gear and first aid.

### Anatomy and physiology

- Building blocks: cells form tissues, tissues form organs, organs form body systems.
- Four tissue types: epithelial, connective, nerve and muscular.
- The skin is the body's largest organ.
- Systems an esthetician should know: integumentary (skin), circulatory, lymphatic, nervous, muscular, skeletal and endocrine.

### Skin structure

| Layer | Key facts |
| --- | --- |
| Epidermis | Outer layer. No blood vessels. New cells form in the basal layer and rise to the surface. Melanocytes here make melanin. |
| Stratum corneum | Top of the epidermis. Dead, keratin-filled cells. The skin's barrier, and the layer exfoliation removes. |
| Dermis | The "true skin." Blood vessels, nerves, glands, follicles, collagen (firmness) and elastin (stretch). |
| Subcutaneous layer | Fat. Cushions, insulates and stores energy. |

- **Sebaceous glands** make sebum (oil). **Sudoriferous glands** make sweat.
- The **acid mantle** is a film of sebum and sweat with a pH of about 4.5 to 5.5.

### Skin disorders and diseases

| Term | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Macule | Flat, discolored spot (freckle) |
| Papule | Small raised bump, no pus |
| Pustule | Raised bump with pus |
| Vesicle | Small blister with clear fluid |
| Open comedone | Blackhead |
| Closed comedone | Whitehead |
| Hyperpigmentation | Too much melanin in an area |
| Hypopigmentation | Too little melanin in an area |
| Rosacea | Long-lasting redness on the central face. Avoid heat and stimulation. |

- **Contagious conditions** (do not service the area): herpes simplex, tinea, conjunctivitis (pinkeye), impetigo.
- **Skin cancers:** basal cell carcinoma (most common, least severe), squamous cell carcinoma, melanoma (most dangerous).
- **ABCDEs of melanoma:** Asymmetry, Border, Color, Diameter, Evolving.
- An esthetician never diagnoses. Refer the client to a physician.

### Hair

- Growth cycle: **anagen** (growing), **catagen** (transition), **telogen** (resting and shedding).
- Hair removal works best in anagen.
- **Hirsutism** and **hypertrichosis** are terms for excessive hair growth.

### Basic chemistry

- **pH scale:** 0 to 14. Below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, above 7 is alkaline.
- Healthy skin is slightly acidic, about 4.5 to 5.5. Harsh alkaline products strip the acid mantle.
- **Emulsion:** oil and water held together by an emulsifier. Oil-in-water is lighter (lotion). Water-in-oil is heavier (cold cream).
- **AHAs** (glycolic, lactic) are water-soluble and work on the surface. **BHA** (salicylic) is oil-soluble and works inside the pore.

## 5. Domain II: Skin Care and Services (45%)

### Consultation, draping and analysis

- Every service starts with a consultation and intake form: health history, medications, allergies, goals.
- Drape the head and body to protect the client's hair and clothes.
- **Skin type** is inherited: dry, normal, combination, oily. **Skin condition** can change: dehydrated, sensitive, acne-prone.
- **Fitzpatrick scale:** Types I to VI by reaction to sun. Type I always burns. Type VI never burns. Higher types are more likely to get dark spots after irritation.
- Analysis tools: magnifying lamp and Wood's lamp.

### Contraindications

Do not perform the service, or change it, when you find:

- Contagious conditions, open sores or sunburn
- Isotretinoin (Accutane) or prescription retinoid use, before waxing or exfoliation
- A pacemaker, metal implants, pregnancy or epilepsy, before electrical treatments
- Recent chemical peels or laser treatments

When unsure, ask for a physician's release.

### Facial steps

A basic facial runs in this order: cleanse, analyze, exfoliate, steam, extract if needed, massage, mask, tone, moisturize, sunscreen. Some schools teach small differences in order, so follow your school's protocol for the practical exam.

- **Exfoliation:** mechanical uses friction (scrubs, brushes, microdermabrasion). Chemical uses acids or enzymes.
- **Massage movements:** effleurage (gliding), petrissage (kneading), tapotement (tapping, the most stimulating), friction (rubbing), vibration (shaking).
- **Masks:** clay or mud for oily skin, cream for dry skin, gel for sensitive skin, rubberized to seal in products, paraffin for warmth.
- **Finish:** moisturizer, sun protection and home care advice.

### Electrical equipment

| Device | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| High frequency | Glass electrode. Warms the skin and kills germs. The direct method is used after extractions. |
| Galvanic | Direct current. Desincrustation (negative pole) softens oil. Iontophoresis pushes products into the skin. |
| Microdermabrasion | Mechanical exfoliation with crystals or a diamond tip plus suction. |
| LED | Light therapy. Blue light is used for acne and red light for aging skin. |
| Steamer | Softens the skin before extractions. Not for rosacea or very sensitive skin. |

### Hair removal

- **Soft wax:** apply thinly in the direction of hair growth. Remove with a strip against the growth, quickly, with the skin held taut.
- **Hard wax:** hardens and is removed without a strip. Gentler on the face.
- Test the wax temperature first. Never double-dip.
- **Tweezing:** hold the skin taut and pull in the direction of growth.

### Makeup

- **Color correcting:** green covers red, peach or orange covers blue, yellow covers purple, lavender covers yellow (sallow) tones.
- Brace your hand near the eyes and lips.
- Use disposable applicators and never dip a used one back into a product.

### Lashes and brows

- Strip lashes: measure, trim the band, let the adhesive get tacky, place at the lash line.
- Lash and brow tinting are within the New Mexico esthetician scope.

- Run each section with a timer.

## 7. New Mexico state law

- **Who's in charge:** the Board of Barbers and Cosmetologists, part of the Regulation and Licensing Department.
- **Where the rules are:** the Barbers and Cosmetologists Act (NMSA 1978, Chapter 61, Article 17A) and Title 16, Chapter 34 of the New Mexico Administrative Code (16.34 NMAC).
- **School hours:** 600 total. Theory 125, sterilization and sanitation 75, facials 350, salon business 50.
- **Scope of practice:** facials and skin treatments, light therapy, high frequency, makeup, lash extensions, lash and brow tinting, and hair removal except electrolysis, invasive methods and shaving.
- **Not allowed:** invasive or medical procedures, and using the title "medical esthetician."
- The exam rewards knowing where a rule is written. Practice looking things up in 16.34 NMAC.
